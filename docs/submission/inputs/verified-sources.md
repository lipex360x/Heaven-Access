## S01 - Workplace tailgating: the principal problem example

**Harvard reference:** The Newsroom (2018) ‘“Smartly dressed” youths foiled in brazen RBS bike theft’, *Edinburgh Evening News*, 3 May. Available at: https://fallback.jpi.prod.brightsites.co.uk/news/crime/smartly-dressed-youths-foiled-in-brazen-rbs-bike-theft-586087 (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Publisher fallback article opened; byline, date and incident narrative read. It describes people accompanying pass-holding staff into the RBS office complex, followed by an interrupted attempt to steal bicycles.

**Use/limit:** Shows a reported workplace bypass of credential-controlled entry. It does not measure prevalence, show card failure or prove that QR passes prevent tailgating. Omit the clothing allegation from the argument because the article records the bank disputing it. The title is retained faithfully only in the reference.

## S02 - Irish office-security consequences, with a different entry mechanism

**Harvard reference:** Data Protection Commissioner (2012) *Twenty-Third Annual Report of the Data Protection Commissioner 2011*, ‘Theft of Revenue Laptops’, pp. 16–17. Available at: https://www.dataprotection.ie/sites/default/files/uploads/2018-12/AnnualReport2011.pdf (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Official 74-page PDF opened and downloaded for text inspection. The foreword is signed in April 2012, supporting publication-year 2012 rather than confusing it with reporting-year 2011. Printed pp. 16–17 describe ten encrypted laptops stolen after forced entry on 27 January 2011, later recovered, and physical-security concerns.

**Use/limit:** A historical example of consequences for office/information security, not an unauthorized-visitor/elevator incident, a GDPR enforcement case, or proof that Heaven Access would have prevented the theft. Encryption was present; do not report an established disclosure of the laptop data.

## S03 - GDPR: the EU legal text

**Harvard reference:** European Parliament and Council of the European Union (2016) ‘Regulation (EU) 2016/679’, *Official Journal of the European Union*, L119, pp. 1–88. Available at: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/pdfs/eur_20160679_adopted_en.pdf (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Opened the original EU Official Journal PDF preserved by the UK National Archives. Articles 5, 6 and 13 were inspected for principles, legal grounds and information duties. The document identifies Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and the 4 May 2016 Official Journal. It is the adopted EU text, **not a substitute UK-GDPR regime**.

**Use/limit:** Supports minimum necessary data, limited retention, security, a justified legal ground and transparent information. EUR-Lex attempts were challenged/unavailable; this readable official-publication copy was used instead. Current DPC guidance below corroborates the relevant Irish/EU application. No claim that all subsequent amendments or every GDPR obligation were audited is made.

## S04 - Irish statutory context

**Harvard reference:** Ireland (2018) *Data Protection Act 2018*, No. 7 of 2018. Revised text maintained by the Law Reform Commission, updated to 16 July 2026. Available at: https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2018/act/7/revised/en/html (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Opened the Law Reform Commission's administrative consolidation; Act identity, revision date and long title read. The Act gives further effect to GDPR and establishes the Data Protection Commission.

**Use/limit:** Identifies the Irish framework alongside GDPR. It is not used as evidence for any specific visitor-log retention duration. The enacted Irish Statute Book link returned 403; use this inspected consolidation, with its stated revision scope, rather than claiming that inaccessible page was read.

## S05 - Selecting a processing basis

**Harvard reference:** Data Protection Commission (n.d.-e) ‘Lawful processing’. Available at: https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/know-your-obligations/lawful-processing (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Official page opened; Article 6 grounds and the qualification on legitimate interests read.

**Use/limit:** Building security may be a purpose for assessing legitimate interests; suitability is a project recommendation to assess, not a DPC decision approving this service or a claim that consent is always necessary.

## S06 - Notice content

**Harvard reference:** Data Protection Commission (n.d.-f) ‘Transparency’. Available at: https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/know-your-obligations/transparency (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Official guidance opened and its information list read: organisation, purpose/basis, disclosures, retention and rights; conditional information includes legitimate interests and international transfers.

**Use/limit:** Supports improving the actual prototype notice for a real operator. A notice acknowledgement is not a lawful basis. A live controller/contact, approved basis and retention policy have not been established for this academic prototype.

## S07 - Practical minimisation, access and retention

**Harvard reference:** Data Protection Commission (n.d.-c) ‘Data Security Guidance’. Available at: https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/know-your-obligations/data-security-guidance (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Opened the corrected working URL and read ‘Data collection and retention policies’ and ‘Access control’. The earlier shorter /data-security URL was not usable.

**Use/limit:** Supports keeping data only while needed and limiting access to operational need. This page contains legacy statute wording; use its practical advice, with GDPR in S03 as the legal foundation. It does not prove the prototype has an automatic deletion job or production-grade permissions.

## S08 - Whether a DPIA is necessary

**Harvard reference:** Data Protection Commission (n.d.-b) ‘Data Protection Impact Assessments’. Available at: https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/know-your-obligations/data-protection-impact-assessments (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Official guidance opened; ‘How do I know if a DPIA should be conducted?’ and the high-risk criteria read.

**Use/limit:** Assess actual nature, scale and risks before live processing; do not label every QR camera a biometric system or automatically exempt it from assessment. No completed DPIA is claimed. Facial identification/cross-building matching would change the assessment, but neither is implemented.

## S09 - Hosting and responsibility

**Harvard reference:** Data Protection Commission (n.d.-a) ‘Controller and Processor relationships’. Available at: https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/know-your-obligations/controller-and-processor-relationships (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Official guidance opened; purpose/means distinction and data-processing-contract discussion read.

**Use/limit:** A live deployment must identify the actual operator and review hosting/processor arrangements. Vercel/Neon availability or a London region does not prove compliant contractual arrangements, exclusively EEA processing or lawful transfers. Those provider arrangements were not legally audited here.

## S10 - Acknowledgement is not universal consent

**Harvard reference:** Data Protection Commission (n.d.-d) ‘Is my consent required for my data to be processed?’. Available at: https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/faqs/general/my-consent-required-my-data-be-processed (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Official FAQ opened and its explanation that consent is not required for every purpose read.

**Use/limit:** Supports the project’s accepted notice-acknowledgement interaction and its separation from the eventual processing basis. It does not select that basis for Heaven Access.

## S11 - Professional code applied to actual features

**Harvard reference:** Association for Computing Machinery (2018) *ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct*, principles 1.2, 1.3 and 1.6. Available at: https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Direct automated requests received 403. The canonical page was then successfully opened in Chrome through web-view. The actual sections on avoiding harm, honest capability/limitation claims and privacy were read, along with the 22 June 2018 adoption statement. This is browser-verified content, not a citation inferred from search snippets. The PDF-booklet URL was blocked and is not the selected final link.

**Use/limit:** Connect to local QR-frame processing, avoiding unnecessary fields, staff-assisted access and honest reporting of simulation/test limits. It is a professional code, not legal certification or evidence of measured security improvement.

## S12 - Safe escape remains separate from entry authorisation

**Harvard reference:** Health and Safety Authority (n.d.) ‘Emergency Escape and Fire Fighting’. Available at: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/fire/emergency_escape_and_fire_fighting/ (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Official page opened and the ‘In All Premises’ escape-route guidance read, including availability of escape-route doors without a key.

**Use/limit:** A future physical checkpoint must preserve safe escape. The prototype has no physical egress control, reliable occupancy register or verified lift/fire strategy. Do not promise an ordinary elevator as an evacuation method or a QR requirement for leaving safely.

**Harvard reference:** Joint Task Force (2020) *Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations*. NIST Special Publication 800-53, Revision 5, PE-2, p. 180. Available at: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

**Verification:** Official NIST PDF opened; PE-2 at printed p. 180 inspected for authorisation-list review, removal of unnecessary access and the distinction between permanent credentials and visitors.

**Use/limit:** Optional context for Bella’s recurring-occupant question. This is not an Irish legal mandate, an instruction to add NIST’s other control enhancements, or implemented employee access. Omit this entry from the final bibliography if the final pages do not cite it.

If the final source set changes, adjust suffixes consistently. Use source titles/dates as verified above; an access date is not a publication date. Cite the specific source beside the claim and retain only cited entries in the final reference pages.
